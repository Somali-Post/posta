# Somali Post Design Guide

Read this file before making any website design or visual changes.

## Reference Assets

- `hero-image.png` - primary visual direction for the new site.
- `navlogo.png` - horizontal logo for navigation/header use.
- `logo-so.png` - stacked logo for larger brand moments.
- `somali-post-content-inventory.txt` - content source to read before writing page copy.

## Brand Direction

Somali Post should feel official, modern, national, trustworthy, and service-focused. The visual language should connect postal delivery, Somalia, public infrastructure, route networks, and reliable customer support.

Use the provided illustration style as the direction: clean, bright, optimistic, blue-and-white, with Somali postal workers, parcel handling, map routes, location pins, postal buildings, vehicles, and soft national geography cues.

## Color Palette

Use this blue-led palette as the base direction:

- Postal Blue: `#0072E5`
- Bright Service Blue: `#0A84FF`
- Deep Navy: `#002B68`
- Dark Ink Blue: `#071B46`
- Sky Blue: `#BFE4FF`
- Pale Sky: `#EAF6FF`
- Ice White: `#F7FBFF`
- White: `#FFFFFF`

Use white and pale sky surfaces heavily. Use Postal Blue for primary actions, brand marks, route lines, and key interactive states. Use Deep Navy or Dark Ink Blue for headings and official/institutional contrast.

Avoid purple, violet, neon gradients, dark SaaS styling, generic glass cards, and decorative blur blobs.

## Logo Use

- Use `navlogo.png` where a horizontal lockup is needed, especially navigation.
- Use `logo-so.png` where a centered or stacked brand moment is needed.
- Keep logos on white, ice white, or very pale blue surfaces.
- Do not recolor the logo unless a future official brand asset requires it.
- Keep clear space around the logo; do not crowd it with buttons or dense text.

## Visual Style

- Prefer bright, open compositions over dark layouts.
- Use soft blue route lines, map pins, postal wave motifs, parcel shapes, and Somali map references when useful.
- Illustrations should look specific to Somali Post, not like generic shipping stock art.
- Use official-service clarity: clear hierarchy, calm spacing, readable sections, and direct contact paths.
- Keep design bespoke, but not experimental in a way that makes public-service tasks harder.

## Layout Principles

- Avoid generic centered SaaS hero sections.
- Use asymmetry where it improves the page: copy on one side, service/map/worker visual on the other.
- Avoid repeated identical card grids unless the content genuinely needs scanning.
- Do not nest cards inside cards.
- Use crisp dividers, light panels, and controlled spacing instead of heavy shadows.
- Make tracking, sending guidance, receiving guidance, and contact details easy to find.

## Typography

- Typography should feel official and accessible.
- Use strong, confident headings with clear hierarchy.
- Body copy should be easy to read on mobile and desktop.
- Use tabular numbers for tracking examples, phone numbers, counters, and operational data.

## Interaction

- Motion should be subtle and purposeful: hover feedback, route drawing, small reveal transitions, or tracking-state feedback.
- Avoid decorative animation that distracts from service tasks.
- Always preserve keyboard access, focus states, and readable contrast.

## Content Priorities

Preserve these facts prominently:

- Somali Post is Somalia's national postal service.
- Inbound international postal exchange has been restored.
- BN1011 is an interim routing/postal code, not a final national postcode system.
- Recipient mobile or WhatsApp number is essential.
- Tracking uses 13-character S10 tracking numbers.
- RUG PUDO is planned/under development, not live.
- Contact details and social links must stay easy to find.

## Pre-Design Checklist

Before making design changes:

- Read `DESIGN_GUIDE.md`.
- Read `somali-post-content-inventory.txt` for factual content.
- Check the provided logo and hero image references.
- Confirm the design uses the blue/white Somali Post palette.
- Confirm it does not reproduce the old site style.
